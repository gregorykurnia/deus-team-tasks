# Design brief

## Who uses it
Me (the owner) and my internal team: a small sales-and-operations team at one company. We use it about equally on laptops or desktops at our desks and on our phones when we're out.

## Language
English only.

## Personality
Bright, clean, spacious and visually stunning. Modern and pleasant to look at, with plenty of breathing room.
- **Yes:** current, modern design. Modern sans-serif type. Light, airy surfaces. Polish in the details.
- **No:** anything that looks weird, wacky, experimental, gimmicky or "fun". Nothing that looks traditional, old-fashioned or dated, and no old-fashioned fonts.

## References
The owner asked Claude to pick these three for the look described above. What matters is what they share, not copying any of them.
- **Things 3:** calm and very spacious, clean type, colour used sparingly and on purpose.
- **Notion:** bright and plain, a modern sans-serif, lots of white space, controls that stay quiet until you need them.
- **Stripe Dashboard:** clean and professional, clear hierarchy, crisp numbers and information, tasteful accent colours.

## Constraints
- Phone and large displays matter equally. Neither is an afterthought.
- Installable as an app.
- **Light mode only. No dark mode at any step**, even where the process asks for light and dark.
- No sign-in. Everyone on the team can see and change everything.

## Branding
**Kept:** the company logo, as it is. The file isn't passed to the studio. Use a neutral placeholder where a logo would sit, and the real logo goes in at the rebuild. If the chosen colours clash with the logo, the owner decides at approval.
**Open:** everything else, including colours, type, symbols and how the app is named in the interface.

## Permission to challenge the model
Yes. The designer may propose merging, splitting or removing concepts. Every proposal is flagged for the owner's decision and never adopted silently.
