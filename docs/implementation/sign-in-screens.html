Signing in with a code, and notifications on Audrey’s iPhone

Every new screen for the first phase, drawn with the app’s own colours, letters and parts (design guide v1.0). Phones come first, at 390 wide, then one laptop view. Only people who sign in see any of this. Everyone else uses the app exactly as today.

1Where it starts

One new row in More on the phone, and one line at the foot of the side bar on a laptop. Once signed in, the same place shows who you are.

2Sign in: her email

A short bottom sheet over whatever screen she was on. Only @deushcs.com emails that you have added can get a code.

3Sign in: the 6-digit code

The same sheet moves on to the code. iPhone can’t fill the code from the Titan app by itself, so pasting is made easy: paste all six digits into the first box and they spread across.

4The code email

Sent from a deushcs.com mailbox on Titan, so it comes from your own domain. The code is in the subject, so it already shows in the Titan app’s notification and she may not need to open the email.

5Turn on notifications

On iPhone, notifications only work in the app on the Home Screen, and only after she taps a button and allows them. So this sheet has four versions, depending on where she opens it.

6Her account

Opened from More (phone) or the side bar foot (laptop). Who is signed in, the state of notifications on this device, and sign out.

7What arrives on her iPhone

Two kinds of notification. The words are the design here: the title says what happened, the body names the task and its deadline the way the app does.

8On a laptop

The same sheets float on the right, like the install sheet (460 wide). The side bar’s foot gets “Sign in”, or who is signed in. A laptop browser can take notifications too, without installing.

Defaults I picked

  1. Nothing changes for anyone who doesn’t sign in. Sign in lives only in More and the side bar foot.
  2. Responsible only. Being added to Informed sends nothing.
  3. No self-notifications. Giving yourself a task sends nothing.
  4. The summary skips empty mornings and Friday covers the weekend and Monday.
  5. Done and parked tasks never count. Subtasks count on their own.
  6. The notification doesn’t say who gave the task. Nobody else signs in yet, so the app can’t know. Phase 2 can name who gave it.
  7. Signing out stops notifications on that device. Several devices per person are fine.
  8. “You” and “your tasks” appear only here. The rest of the app keeps the guide’s rule of no “your” (7, rule 2), since nobody else is signed in.
