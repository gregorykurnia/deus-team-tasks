## Deal stages (DEUS default)

1. **First meeting** - presented; needs and current tools known.
2. **Trial agreed** - prospect has agreed to a free trial or pilot, with a named owner on their side.
3. **Trial done and reviewed** - results walked through with the prospect; their evaluator is convinced or has specific objections.
4. **Budget slot** - written into their budget or a purchase request; proposal sent.
5. **Procurement** - vendor registration, PO, contract.
6. **Won** - first paid batch run. Then expansion (more modules, more divisions).

A deal that ends a meeting with "interesting, maybe next year" is still at stage 1, however warm it felt.

## Qualification checklist

Flag only the items that block the next stage.

- **Buyer type:** recruitment, talent management (promotion, high-potential), L&D, or performance management. Each has a different budget owner and volume.
- **Pain:** what isn't working today, in their words. "Open to new methods" is not a pain; "we're not satisfied with our personality results" is.
- **Current tools / incumbent:** in-house platform, psychology bureau, other vendors. Don't sell what they already do well.
- **Volume:** people assessed per year. Drives pricing and which package fits.
- **Workflow fit:** do they assess in batches or one person at a time, and where would DEUS results go in their process (shortlisting, assessment-center interview, promotion panel, development plan)? One-by-one buyers need the individual and promotion reports, not the batch ranking; batch buyers need the summary and suitability views.
- **Champion:** the person who will push internally. **Evaluator:** often an in-house psychologist or assessor team who will test rigour.
- **Decision maker and budget cycle:** who signs, when next year's budget is submitted and approved. Missing this window delays a deal by a year.
- **Procurement:** vendor registration, data security or legal review requirements.
- **Module fit:** which module answers their pain, which they don't need.

## Common objections and good answers

| They say | What's behind it | Good answer |
|---|---|---|
| "We already have cognitive tests" | No gap there | Agree; don't push cognitive. Lead with what they lack (usually Neuro / Drive & Risk or behavioural personality). |
| "Our trait definitions are different" | Vendor constructs don't map to their competency dictionary | Offer a construct map: each DEUS dimension → their definition, and customize competencies to their framework. |
| "What theory is this based on?" / "How was it validated?" | Expert evaluator testing rigour | Name the theory and paradigm per game, the 4-phase development, Indonesian norms, Ansevana. Give numbers if we have them; if not, promise the paper by a date and deliver. Never improvise. |
| "Is it adaptive / IRT?" | Psychometric sophistication | Fixed items by design for fairness in a game format; scores are compared against the same item set and Indonesian norms. |
| "People can fake personality tests" (or we raise it) | Self-report weakness | Forced ranking of 4 good options in a story context; games capture behaviour, not self-description. |
| "Budget is closed this year" | Timing, not rejection | Run the trial now so results exist when next year's budget is written. Ask when the submission window closes. |
| "Our volume is small / minimum is too high" | One-by-one assessments | Token bundle sized to their real annual volume; personality per unit. Exceptions need internal approval before quoting. |
| "Results look made for recruitment" | Talent-management buyer | Show the individual report and internal promotion report with interview guide. |
| "Candidates' internet is a problem" | Delivery risk | Browser-based, no install; Telkom ran 30+ provinces at once. |

## Trial playbook

A generic free trial proves little. Design it to answer the evaluator's real question.

1. **Assessor run:** their assessors or HR team play the module themselves first. Expert buyers trust what they've experienced.
2. **Comparison batch:** 10-30 people they have already assessed with their own tools take the module. DEUS compares its results with their existing results and shows where they agree and what DEUS adds.
3. **Results walkthrough** with the evaluator and the champion together. Bring the construct map.
4. **Decision ask:** agree what happens if results convince them (budget slot, pilot size, date).

Time the trial to finish before the prospect's budget submission. Do the light step first (assessor run, about 30 minutes per person, fits even a busy month) and the heavy step (comparison batch with real employees) when their team has room.

## Timing rules (Indonesia)

- **Budget cycle:** many companies plan next year's budget in Q3-Q4 and lock it before year-end; some submit in Q1. Never assume: always ask when the next submission closes, and plan backwards from it.
- **Year-end:** mid-December to early January is slow (closing, holidays). Avoid scheduling sessions or batches there; get commitments for January before it starts.
- **Ramadan and Lebaran:** the fasting month runs at reduced pace, and the weeks around Lebaran (Eid al-Fitr) are close to a standstill, with many people on leave. Check this year's dates before planning; don't put a decision meeting or a large batch in the Lebaran week.
- **Lead time:** allow 1-2 weeks to prepare materials (technical brief, construct map, sample reports) before an expert audience sees anything.
- **Proposed dates:** offer two concrete weekday options, never "sometime next week".

## Follow-up message rules

- Within 24 hours of the meeting.
- Same channel and language the prospect used; polite Indonesian (Bu/Pak) by default for Indonesian prospects.
- Short: thanks, 2-3 points they cared about, the next step with 2 date options, promised materials.
- No new prices, discounts or claims beyond the meeting and `deus-offer.md`.

## Worked example (Mayora Indah, Nov 2026)

Talent-management buyer (supervisor+ promotions and high-potential selection), in-house cognitive platform, unsatisfied with personality and drive measures, budget closed for 2026, open to a trial.

- Lead offer: Neuro / Drive & Risk only; don't push cognitive; personality blocked by definition mapping.
- Top gaps: technical credibility (theory, numbers, verbal/numerical answer), construct map, trial designed as assessor run + comparison batch, 2027 budget window unknown, conflicting minimums, champion not yet named.
- Side opportunity: the HR head's private practice wanted Drive & Risk at a Rp3-5M minimum; a small yes builds a hands-on advocate.
- Earliest realistic paid start: Q1 2027, if the trial lands before budget submission.
